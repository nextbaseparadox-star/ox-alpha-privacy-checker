    01Where these facts come from

    Every claim above is quoted from OpenRouter's own model page for stealth/ox-alpha and its public API, read on 20 August 2026.

    OpenRouter's wording: "This stealth model is developed and operated by a third-party model provider. Prompts and completions for this model are retained by the provider and are not used for training; all other use is governed by the Stealth Model Terms."

    And: "It is developed and operated by a third-party provider who has chosen to remain anonymous during this preview. OpenRouter routes requests to it and is not its developer, owner, or provider."

    02How the score is built

    Each data type carries a weight reflecting what disclosure would actually cost you — credentials and customer records score highest because the damage is immediate and not undoable. The weights are summed, capped at 100, and banded.

    This is a judgement, not a standard. It is meant to make the trade-off explicit, not to replace your own risk assessment or your employer's policy.

    03What is not being claimed

    Nothing here says the provider is untrustworthy. An anonymous provider is not the same as a bad actor, and stealth previews are a normal way to test a model before launch.

    The point is narrower: you cannot evaluate a data policy when you do not know whose it is, and "retained" plus "unknown jurisdiction" is a combination worth deciding about deliberately rather than by default.
